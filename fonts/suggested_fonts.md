# KomaSCAD font collection and suggestions

The six font files beside this guide are original, unmodified upstream releases. Each is licensed under the [SIL Open Font License 1.1](https://openfontlicense.org/), which permits free commercial and non-commercial use and redistribution when the accompanying license notice is retained. The matching notices are in [licenses](licenses).

All bundled fonts were checked for the `王` and `将` glyphs. Font coverage varies for less common historical characters, so confirm the full set of characters required by a design before committing to a family.

## Additional open options

- [Noto Serif JP](https://fonts.google.com/noto/specimen/Noto+Serif+JP) is a dependable formal fallback. It is clean and highly legible, though less brush-like than Yuji Syuku.
- [Klee One](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Klee+One) is an OFL handwriting font worth testing for informal labels and maker signatures.
- [Shippori Mincho](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Shippori+Mincho) is an OFL formal Mincho family for a quieter, book-printed appearance.

## Interesting Options

The reference pieces are closest to traditional shogi *koma-ji*, especially the flowing **Ryoko (菱湖)** family of forms, with some of the strength of **Kinki (錦旗)**. They are less like ordinary typesetting than a carefully adapted Kaisho/Gyosho hand. These options were selected for their expressive thick-to-thin strokes, long controlled sweeps, sharp hane, dignified structure, and suitability for two vertically arranged kanji. This is research, not a license clearance. Test `王将`, `玉将`, `飛車`, `角行`, `金将`, `銀将`, `桂馬`, `香車`, and `歩兵` before buying a family.

The NKS25 and K'sShogiPieces entries are shogi symbol fonts rather than normal Japanese Unicode text fonts. They may be the closest visual references, but use their documented piece-glyph mapping rather than ordinary Japanese text. The other entries should accept ordinary Japanese text when the selected package contains the advertised Japanese character set. Installed family/style names can differ from the marketing names; check the installed-font list provided by the operating system or design application.

The four traditional shogi families themselves—Ryoko, Kinki, Minase, and Genbei Kiyoyasu—remain the proper visual benchmark. [Issei Kobo's illustrated typeface guide](https://syougigoma-issei.com/typeface/) compares those traditions and many rarer piece hands. If none of the installable fonts reaches the quality of the reference, tracing or commissioning a small, purpose-built set of the required front/back piece inscriptions from an appropriate historical model will likely outperform further generic-font searching.
